10 Things You Must Check Before
Hiring a House Maid in Nigeria

Hiring domestic help in Nigeria requires more than a phone interview. From NIN verification to reference checks, here's a complete checklist every employer should follow before bringing someone into their home.

Adaeze Okafor

10 August 2026 · 7 min read · 2,841 views

Share:

A clean, well-kept home is the goal — hiring the right person gets you there. Photo: Unsplash

This guide is for Nigerian families and employers who are about to hire a domestic worker for the first time — or who want to do it more safely and professionally than before.

Hiring someone to work inside your home is one of the most personal decisions you'll make. Unlike hiring for a corporate role, the domestic hire lives and works in your private space — around your children, your property, and your daily routines. The stakes are high.

Yet many Nigerian families still rely on word-of-mouth alone, or rush the process because they're in urgent need. The result is often avoidable: theft, conflicts, or worse. This checklist will help you slow down, do it properly, and hire with confidence.

1 Verify Their NIN (National Identity Number)

The National Identification Number is the most basic form of identity verification available in Nigeria. Every Nigerian adult should have one. Before you do anything else, ask for the candidate's NIN and verify it through the NIMC MobileID app or by visiting a NIMC enrolment centre.

A verified NIN tells you the person is who they say they are. It also creates a paper trail — which matters enormously if anything goes wrong later.

2 Run a Background Check

A background check goes deeper than an ID verification. It looks at the candidate's history — criminal record, previous employers, any reported incidents. In Nigeria, you can request a police clearance certificate directly from your nearest police station, or use a verified third-party background screening service.

If you hire through SelectRoyal Maids, this step is handled for you. Every professional on our platform has completed a background check before their profile goes live.

  • Police clearance certificate (not older than 6 months)
  • Previous employer reference calls (at least 2)
  • Online name search to flag any public incidents
  • Confirm address matches NIN records

3 Call At Least Two Former Employers

References matter. A CV tells you what someone wants you to know. A phone call with a previous employer tells you what they actually experienced. Ask specific questions — not just "was she a good worker?" but:

  • How long did she/he work for you?
  • Why did they leave?
  • Was there ever an incident involving money, property, or safety?
  • Would you hire them again?

If a candidate cannot provide at least two contactable references, that is a red flag worth taking seriously.

4 Conduct a Structured Interview

Many employers interview domestic staff the same way they'd chat with a neighbour — casually and without structure. This leads to poor hiring decisions. Instead, prepare a consistent set of questions that you ask every candidate:

Experience

"Describe your last household. How many people lived there? What were your main duties?"

Values

"If you found ₦5,000 on the kitchen counter, what would you do?"

Problem-Solving

"A child falls and cuts themselves while you're alone — what's your first action?"

Commitment

"What are your long-term goals, and how does this role fit into them?"

5 Assess Their Relevant Skills

For a cook, ask them to prepare a simple meal during the interview. For a nanny, arrange a supervised interaction with your child. For a house cleaner, walk them through a room and ask how they'd approach it. Practical assessment removes guesswork entirely.

Skills to assess based on the role:

  • Maid / House Help: Cleaning, laundry, ironing, organisation
  • Cook: Cooking technique, hygiene, meal planning
  • Nanny: Child engagement, first aid awareness, patience
  • Driver: Valid licence, road knowledge, vehicle care
  • Elderly Carer: Patience, mobility assistance, medication awareness

6 Agree on Clear Terms Before They Start

Verbal agreements are the root cause of most domestic staff disputes in Nigeria. Before anyone starts work, write down — and sign — an agreement covering at minimum:

  • Monthly salary and payment date
  • Daily working hours and days off
  • Duties and responsibilities (be specific)
  • Living arrangements if live-in
  • Notice period for either party
  • Rules on use of phone, visitors, and personal time

7 Discuss Salary Honestly and Fairly

Underpaying domestic staff is not just unfair — it creates resentment and drives theft. Research the current going rate in your city for the role you're hiring. Lagos rates differ from Abuja, which differ from Port Harcourt.

8 Set Up a Probationary Period

Never go straight from interview to permanent hire. A 30 to 90-day probationary period protects both sides. During this time:

  • Monitor performance against the agreed duties
  • Secure valuables and sensitive documents as you would normally
  • Check in weekly to give and receive feedback
  • Observe how they interact with children or elderly family members

At the end of the probationary period, hold a brief review. If things are going well, confirm the hire in writing. If not, part ways professionally and start again.

9 Install Basic Home Security

This is not about distrust — it's about creating a safe environment for everyone. Basic security measures protect your household and protect an honest worker from being falsely accused.

Recommended Before Day One

Install at least one visible camera in common areas like the living room or entrance. Keep valuables in a lockable drawer or cabinet. Inform your new hire about the security setup — transparency reduces anxiety on both sides.

10 Treat Them With Dignity

The best domestic professionals choose their employers carefully — and they leave bad ones quickly. If you want someone who is committed, hardworking, and loyal, the single most important thing you can do is treat them with basic human dignity.

This means:

  • Paying on time, every time
  • Providing adequate food and rest
  • Not shouting or demeaning in front of others
  • Acknowledging good work
  • Giving reasonable time off and sick leave

A household that is known to be a good place to work will never struggle to find quality staff. Word travels fast in domestic professional communities.

The Bottom Line

Hiring domestic help should be a deliberate, structured process — not something you rush through out of desperation. Follow this checklist and you'll reduce your risk dramatically, build a healthier working relationship, and protect your home and family.
